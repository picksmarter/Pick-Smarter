About Pick Smarter

A sports fan, an Excel spreadsheet, and one question: How do I make decisions based on math and statistics?

Pick Smarter was built to make Pick'em strategy easier to understand. It compares estimated win probability with how the public is actually picking, then turns that difference into simple recommendations you can use before your picks lock.

The $100 Experiment
Turned $10 → $100

I won my work's March Madness pool, without watching a single college basketball game that season.

And I used math to do it.

There was a $100 prize on the line, and I knew almost nothing about that year's college basketball season. I watched a grand total of zero games that season. So instead of pretending I knew every team and guessing, I approached the bracket like a math problem.

I looked at win probabilities, the percentage of people who were picking each team to advance, and tried to make selections that gave me a better path to finishing ahead of everyone else.

I ended up winning the pool. By a lot too. My coworkers' brackets were in shambles — the over-picked Duke lost in the Elite Eight. But my champion, Michigan, was still playing. They ended up winning the whole thing, and I turned that $10 into $100.

That didn't prove I could predict the future. It showed me something much more interesting: winning a pool is not always the same problem as simply picking the most likely winner.

That idea stuck with me, and it eventually became the foundation for Pick Smarter.

What Pick Smarter actually does

Most people approach Pick'em like a prediction contest: pick the team you think is most likely to win and move on.

But you're not playing against the games, you're playing against everyone else in the pool. That means what everyone else is picking matters too.

You don't need to predict the future perfectly. You just need to predict it better than everyone else.

Pick Smarter compares two numbers for every matchup:

Estimated Win %

How likely the team is to win based on sportsbook market probabilities after removing the sportsbook's built-in margin.

Public Pick %

How often people in the relevant Pick'em contest are actually selecting that team.

When those numbers disagree, there may be an opportunity.

A team can be favored but overvalued. Another team can be an underdog while still having a much better chance to win than the public's picks suggest. Pick Smarter highlights those situations and turns them into one clear recommendation for each matchup.

The goal isn't to be different just for the sake of being different. It's to make smarter decisions about when following the crowd makes sense, and when it may be worth going another direction.

Why I built Pick Smarter

I've always loved sports, especially college football and the NFL, but the part that really hooked me was the strategy behind making decisions.

Over time, I started building spreadsheets and formulas, comparing probabilities, looking at public pick percentages, and testing different ways to think about Pick'em contests, Fantasy Football, and March Madness Brackets. What started as something I was doing for myself gradually became a system I wanted to keep improving, and to teach others what I have learned.

Then the results started getting interesting.

I began seeing strong results in the contests where I used this way of thinking, including some of my best finishes in large Pick'em fields.

  • 2025 College Pick'Em 99.5% · 413th place of 66,444 View result →
  • 2025 March Madness 98.5% · 24,934th of 1,539,164 View result →
  • 2026 CFP Bracket Challenge 99.5% · 7,821th of 133,466 View result →

Those results made me want to understand the idea better, not hide it behind a “secret formula.”

I wanted a tool that could show the numbers, explain why a recommendation was being made, admit when a pick lost, and make the strategy understandable to someone who doesn't want to spend hours building Excel spreadsheets.

I couldn't find exactly what I wanted, so I started building it.

Who's behind the spreadsheet?

I'm Carter, the person building Pick Smarter.

I'm not a professional statistician, sportsbook trader (sportsbooks are designed to take your money), or someone claiming to know the outcome of every game. I'm a sports fan who happens to really enjoy statistics, probability, spreadsheets, entrepreneurship, and figuring out better ways to make decisions.

That combination is basically how Pick Smarter happened.

I built the first version because it was something I wanted to use myself. Now I'm putting it in front of other Pick'em players, publishing the methodology, tracking the results publicly, and improving it based on what people actually find useful.

A few things I believe

  • Good picks can lose.
  • Popular picks can still be the right picks.
  • Being contrarian doesn't automatically make you smart.
  • If a recommendation can't be explained clearly, then it's worth nothing at all.

Where it's headed

Pick Smarter is starting with College Football and NFL Pick'em because weekly contests are the best place to prove whether this idea is actually useful. But the long-term vision goes beyond giving everyone the exact same list of picks.

Next

Personalized Pick'em recommendations

The goal is to eventually let your situation change the recommendation. Including things like pool size, current standing, and how much risk you actually need to take.

Someone protecting a lead should not necessarily receive the same advice as someone trying to make up ground.

March

March Madness

When March Madness arrives, I want to bring the same probability vs public thinking to the bracket challenge. Where pool size, popular teams, and how the rest of your group builds its bracket can matter enormously.

Exploring

Fantasy football tools

There are also ideas I want to experiment with in fantasy football, including smarter draft boards and start/sit tools that use market information as another useful signal.

Those are experiments, not promises. Pick Smarter will expand only when there's a real problem worth solving and a useful way to solve it.

No locks. No guarantees. No black box.

Pick Smarter is a decision-support tool. Sports are unpredictable, that's why we find them so interesting. Probabilities are just that, probabilities. A good decision will sometimes produce bad results, like how Michigan lost to App State in 2007.

That's why every public Pick Smarter recommendation is meant to be recorded before the game, left unchanged after the result is known, and judged honestly over time.

The methodology is public. The track record includes the misses. And as the system changes, I want those changes to be explained instead of hidden.

The goal isn't to pretend uncertainty and variance doesn't exist. It's to make better decisions within it.
