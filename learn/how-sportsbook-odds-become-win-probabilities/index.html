How Sportsbook Odds Become Win Probabilities

Win % on the Pick Smarter dashboard starts life as a sportsbook moneyline. Getting from one to the other takes two steps: converting the moneyline to an implied probability, then removing the sportsbook's margin.

Step 1: Moneyline to implied probability

A moneyline is simply a payout structure written as odds. A negative moneyline (like -190) tells you how much you'd need to bet to win $100; a positive moneyline (like +160) tells you how much you'd win on a $100 bet. Converting either format into an implied probability is a standard, well-known calculation — the math itself isn't proprietary, and you'll find the same conversion formulas published across the sports betting industry.

Step 2: Removing the vig

Here's the catch: if you convert both sides of a matchup to implied probability and add them together, they won't sum to exactly 100%. They'll sum to a bit more — typically somewhere in the low single digits over. That extra percentage is the sportsbook's built-in margin, the vig (short for "vigorish"), and it's how the book makes money regardless of the outcome.

Because that margin is baked into both sides, neither raw implied probability is a fair estimate on its own. To fix that, we scale both sides down proportionally so they sum to exactly 100% — a standard, widely used method often called "de-vigging" or removing the vig. The result is what Pick Smarter shows as Win %: our best fair estimate of each team's true chance to win, with the sportsbook's margin stripped out.

Why this matters for Pick'em

Using the raw, un-devigged number would systematically overstate both teams' chances (since the two sides sum to more than 100%), which would throw off every downstream calculation — especially Leverage, which depends on Win % being a fair, apples-to-apples number.
