Small Pool vs. Large Pool Strategy

Leverage matters in every pool, but how aggressively you should lean into it isn't fixed — it depends heavily on how many people you're competing against. A 10-person pool with your coworkers and a 10,000-person national pool call for meaningfully different mindsets, even though the underlying numbers on the dashboard are identical.

Small pools: differentiation is harder to come by, and less necessary

In a small pool, there simply aren't enough entries for the public pick percentages to represent a huge, faceless crowd — the "public" number is really just a handful of people. You typically need fewer distinguishing picks to separate yourself, and a single bad week is more survivable relative to a small field. Leaning too hard into low-probability, high-leverage picks in a small pool can be riskier than it needs to be, because you don't need as much separation to compete.

Large pools: the crowd is the opponent

In a large pool, the "public" percentage is a much more reliable signal of real crowd behavior, and correct-but-obvious picks do very little to move you up a large field. Here, well-chosen leverage picks matter more, because differentiating from thousands of entries generally takes more than just being right — it takes being right about something the field largely missed.

What doesn't change

In both cases, the same principle from Why Being Contrarian Isn't Automatically Smart still applies: leverage without a real probability behind it is just risk. Pool size changes how much leverage is worth chasing — it doesn't change whether a pick needs to be grounded in an actual win probability edge.
