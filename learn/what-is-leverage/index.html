What Is Leverage in Pick'em?

In a Pick'em pool, picking correctly isn't quite the same as picking well. If you take the same favorite everyone else is taking, getting it right doesn't separate you from the field — everyone moves up together. Leverage is the idea that measures how much a pick can actually separate you from the pack, relative to how likely it is to work.

The definition

Pick Smarter defines leverage as a simple gap:

Leverage = Win % − Public %

If a team's real win probability is 55% but only 30% of the public is picking it, that team has +25 points of leverage — its true chance of winning is notably better than the credit it's getting from the field. If a team is a 75% favorite but 92% of the public is on it, that team has −17 points of leverage — it's likely to win, but a correct pick there barely separates you from anyone else.

Why leverage matters more in pools than straight betting

If you were simply trying to predict the most winners, you'd always take the higher win probability. But most Pick'em pools reward you relative to everyone else, not in isolation. A correct pick that almost everyone else also got right does very little for your standing. A correct pick that most of the field got wrong can be worth far more. Leverage is a way of quantifying that difference before the games are played.

Leverage isn't the whole story

High leverage on its own doesn't make a pick good — it usually just means the win probability is being under-credited by the public. A team with a 20% chance to win and 2% public ownership still loses 4 times out of 5. That's why Pick Smarter never recommends a team on leverage alone; leverage is combined with win probability to produce the final Pick Smarter Pick.
