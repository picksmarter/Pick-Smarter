Win Probability vs. Public Pick Percentage

These two numbers sit right next to each other on the dashboard, and it's easy to glance at them as if they're measuring the same thing. They're not. One describes the game. The other describes the people playing it.

Win % describes the game

Win % is our estimate of a team's actual chance to win, built from sportsbook odds with the bookmaker's built-in margin (the vig) removed. It doesn't know or care what anyone is picking — it's purely about the matchup. See How Sportsbook Odds Become Win Probabilities for exactly how that conversion works.

Public % describes the pool

Public % is how often real Pick'em entries are actually selecting that team. It has nothing to do with the game itself — it's a measurement of crowd behavior: name recognition, recency bias, how appealing a team "feels" to pick, media coverage, and plenty of factors that have no bearing on who actually wins.

Why the gap is the useful part

Because these two numbers measure different things, they don't have to move together — and usually they don't move together perfectly. A team can have a strong true win probability while being under-picked because it's less famous, coming off a bad-looking loss, or playing a marquee opponent. A team can have a mediocre true win probability while being over-picked because it's a popular brand or "feels" safe. That gap between the two is exactly what Leverage measures.

Neither number alone tells you what to do

Win % alone ignores the field. Public % alone ignores the game. Pick Smarter exists specifically because the useful information lives in the relationship between the two, not in either one read on its own.
